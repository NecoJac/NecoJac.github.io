GRAD

Geometric Rasterization as Data Augmentation for Driving

VITA, EPFL

CodeCode and data release in preparation

Abstract

Simulated driving states need visual observations, but photorealistic Gaussian splatting (GS) requires costly scene-specific appearance fitting. Building on RAP's CPU-based geometric rasterization, GRAD addresses the visual gap through task-supervised paired pretraining, Advantage Mutual Distillation (AMD), and shared planning, with camera-only deployment. Matched-scene comparisons show that geometric rasters achieve planning performance comparable to GS, while combining the observations further improves planning. Our results show that simplified geometry can serve as both an alternative and a complement to appearance-rich training data.

Method

Paired perception with AMD learns complementary camera and BEV representations from real/GS and raster observations. Shared planning trains a common planner and scorer across both domains. A controller adapter improves NAVSIM execution, while GRPO post-training uses AlpaSim execution feedback. Deployment retains only the camera-domain path.

Learning complementary geometry

Pairing geometric and appearance-rich observations improves planning and real-image road prediction. On the same validation set, paired pretraining raises real-image road IoU from 58.47 to 59.22.

Aligning planning and execution

A safe desired trajectory can become unsafe after controller execution. GRAD corrects the controller reference so that the executed path follows the intended plan more closely.

Closed-loop driving

AlpaSim GRPO post-training raises the public-scenario mean score from 91.92 to 96.17, compared with 91.87 for the released Garage baseline.

V1 uses scorer ensembling; V2 uses a single model. AlpaSim evaluation uses the public 1,475-scenario nuPlan set.
